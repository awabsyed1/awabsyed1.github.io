My First Venture: Lessons from Starting a Robotics Startup

Posted on: March 15, 2024

Starting your first venture is always a mix of excitement, ambition, and reality checks. For me, that first leap was into robotics — a field I was passionate about and eager to build something meaningful in.

Why Robotics?

I chose robotics because it combined my background in mechanical and control systems with a growing interest in AI. The idea was to make technology practical and accessible, not just experimental.

Challenges I Faced

  • Technical Hurdles: Early prototypes were often unstable or unreliable, teaching me the importance of iteration and testing.
  • Business Knowledge: I underestimated how much time and effort goes into business development, customer discovery, and funding compared to the engineering work.
  • Team Building: Finding the right people who share your vision but bring different skills is harder than it looks.

Lessons Learned

The biggest takeaway was that failure isn’t the end — it’s feedback. Each setback was an opportunity to pivot, refine the idea, or simply learn what not to do next time. I also learned that customers care less about “cool tech” and more about how it solves their actual problem.

Looking Ahead

This venture didn’t turn into a unicorn, but it became the foundation for how I approach projects and business ideas today. It gave me perspective on combining technology with entrepreneurship and set the stage for my future goals.

Every engineer or entrepreneur should try building something from scratch at least once. The lessons will stay with you far longer than the product itself.
